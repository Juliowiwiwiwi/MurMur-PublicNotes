1. The Entryway (Login & Onboarding)

Inspired by the first image (the "Notely" splash screen).

    The UI: A clean, welcoming screen with a nice illustration, your app's title, and a short description ("Share your thoughts with the world").

    The Action: Instead of a complex email/password flow, there’s just a single <TextInput> for a "Username" and a "Let's Start" button.

    Under the Hood: When they click start, we save that username to AsyncStorage so they stay logged in, and anonymously authenticate them with Firebase.

2. The Main Hub (Home Feed)

Inspired by the third image (the social feed) and the second image (date sorting).

    The Header: A friendly greeting at the top: "Good evening, @[username]".

    The Date Sorter (Crucial Feature): Right below the header, a horizontal scrollable row of dates (like the "Tue 23 Apr" bubbles in your second image). Tapping a date filters the global feed to only show notes posted on that specific day.

    Category Filters: A row of small pill-shaped buttons (like in the first image) to filter by type: All Notes | Text | Images | Audio.

    The Feed (FlatList): A scrollable list of note cards. Each card displays:

        The author's @username and a timestamp (e.g., "15m ago").

        The content (text preview, a thumbnail if it's an image, or a mini waveform/play button if it's audio).

        A comment icon with the total number of comments.

    The "Add" Button: A Floating Action Button (FAB) anchored to the bottom right (like the + Create or pencil icon in your images) that opens the creation screen.

3. The Creation Engine ("Add Stuff" Page)

    The UI: A clean, distraction-free modal that slides up.

    The Inputs:

        A large text area to type their thoughts.

        An Image Button (opens their phone's camera roll).

        An Audio Button (opens a simple microphone interface to record a quick voice memo).

    Under the Hood (Important Tech Note): Because you want images and audio, Firebase Firestore (the database) isn't enough on its own. We will also need to enable Firebase Storage.

        When a user adds a photo/audio, we upload the file to Firebase Storage first.

        Storage gives us back a URL (a web link) to that file.

        We save that URL inside the Firestore database note document alongside the text and username.

4. The Conversation Room (Detail & Comment View)

    The UI: When a user taps any note on the Home feed, it navigates them to a dedicated screen for that specific note.

    The Layout: * Top: The full, expanded note (full-size image, playable audio, complete text).

        Middle: A scrollable list of comments left by other users, showing their @usernames and timestamps.

        Bottom: A sticky input field that stays above the keyboard: "Type a comment..." and a send button.

    Under the Hood: We will create a comments sub-collection in Firebase linked to the specific note's ID so everything stays organized.
